# D-65. Managed file publication without cross-operation isolation

## Context

W-152 found that two concurrent no-overwrite saves can both succeed on Linux: the runtime's `File.Move` implementation checks for absence and then renames, allowing one complete output to replace another. This is a publication limitation, separate from input/output alias detection. The observed failure and the expert review prompt are recorded in `agents/reference/w-152-publication-review.md`.

## Decision

Keep the managed-only D-60 publication design: unique temporary siblings created with `FileMode.CreateNew`, processing and configured flush completed before publication, and `File.Move` or `File.Replace` publication of complete output. Preserve inputs and prior destinations on processing failures, temporary-file cleanup, and conservative alias safeguards. Retain straightforward path-isolation improvements, including intermediate/final link resolution and refusal of unresolvable reparse points.

Cross-operation publication coordination belongs to callers. Do not add native publication calls, named mutexes, cross-process lock files, runtime dependencies, universal file identity detection, hostile-directory guarantees, or transactional filesystem semantics for W-152.

The owner approved replacing F17's existing-destination sentence with:

> A destination detected as existing shall be replaced only when `Overwrite` is explicitly enabled.

The owner also approved adding this limitation to F17 and public documentation:

> File saves are not coordinated across independent operations or processes targeting the same output pathname. Callers requiring exclusive publication must serialize those operations.
>
> `Overwrite = false` rejects destinations detected as existing during the save operation but does not guarantee atomic create-if-absent behavior against concurrent filesystem changes. On affected Unix runtimes, competing saves to an initially absent destination may both succeed, with one completed output replacing another.
>
> Staging and publication protect against partial results and PDF processing failures; they do not provide transactional isolation against concurrent filesystem modifications.

## Consequences

- This supersedes D-60 only where its overwrite wording could imply atomic create-if-absent under concurrent changes; the managed primitives, complete-output publication, alias, ownership, and flush rules remain.
- W-152 revises concurrency tests after this approval. They assert complete output and cleanup, permit one or both competing saves to succeed, and do not require the nondeterministic race to reproduce. Stable existing-destination rejection and processing-failure preservation keep their regression coverage.
- Apply the approved F17 wording, update README and ACCEPTANCE, and clarify public save-option documentation. Under D-47, QUALIFICATION may restate the approved concurrent-filesystem limitation without changing release evidence or gate status. No skill restates a conflicting exclusive-publication rule.
