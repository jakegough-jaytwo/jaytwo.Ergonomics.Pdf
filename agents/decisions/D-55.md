# D-55. Asynchronous opening through an async-capable parser

## Context

Spec R13 and W20 require `OpenAsync` with genuine asynchronous source reads (`reset-plan.md` section 4.3). The parser is synchronous: `PdfCursor` fills its 64 KiB windows by blocking reads deep inside a recursive cross-reference, trailer, and encryption load. W-132 put three ways to the owner on 2026-10-23: rerun the open on a fresh engine after each cache miss (geometric refetch, but an open-time cache above D-49's 1 MiB ceiling for large tables); prefetch the header and tail asynchronously and fall back to blocking reads; or make the cursor and parser async-capable.

## Decision

1. The cursor and parser become async-capable. Every source read on the asynchronous open path is awaited; nothing is parsed twice and no open-time cache exceeds D-49's ceilings.
2. There is one parser implementation. Its reading methods are asynchronous in shape and take the operation's mode; in synchronous mode every await completes inline, the pattern the save and sign paths already use, so synchronous and asynchronous opens produce the same state.
3. Restart-on-miss and prefetch-with-blocking-fallback are rejected.
4. Before the parser is made asynchronous it is split, behavior-preserving, into a lexer, an object parser, a cross-reference reader, and an object-stream reader (W-135), so the conversion touches smaller units.

## Consequences

- W-132 converts the cursor, the parser, and the engine's open path. Inspection after open, the synchronous open, and synchronous saves keep reading synchronously through the source; asynchronous inspection is not part of W-132.
- Parse failures keep their exception types and reported offsets, and D-49's windows, lifetime, and tripwires are unchanged.
- The synchronous parse pays for async state machines that complete inline. Wall-clock time stays reported and ungated (D-49).

## Authority

The owner, 2026-10-23, choosing "Async parser" in W-132's design question, and asking in the same session for the parser to be refactored for maintainability first.
