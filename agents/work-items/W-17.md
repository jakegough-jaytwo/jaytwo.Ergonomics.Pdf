# W-17. G7 large-file workloads

## Problem

The dense test was 8 MiB and 32 MiB. The attachment test copied 200 KiB. The 1,000-page test was a sparse read and did not extract, edit metadata, and sign. The 3 GiB case was sparse, and its metadata-appendix measurement is W-10.

## Why now

Gate G7 is release-blocking. Sparse files do not meet the file-backed workloads.

## Scope and exclusions

File-backed workloads: a 1 GiB dense PDF, a 250 MiB attachment added and extracted, and a 500-page extract, metadata edit, and sign. Record timing. The Technical Lead and QA approve fixture-specific memory budgets before G7 is called met. Do not treat the proposed 256 MiB and 512 MiB figures as already approved; D-20 later approved them as the working budget only.

## Acceptance criteria

Those three workloads complete correctly on real file-backed inputs. Peak memory and timing are recorded. G7 stays unmet until the fixture-specific budgets are approved. `QUALIFICATION.md` section 9.

## Evidence required

Internally tested measurements.

## Closeout

Accepted at the internally tested level, against the D-20 working budget. G7 is not passed. `LargeWorkloadTests` runs three file-backed workloads on `net8.0`, `net6.0`, and `net48`, measured by the new `WorkloadMeter`:

- A 1,073,915,943-byte dense PDF: inspect, metadata edit, and sign, with peak heap growth of at most 3.06 MiB. Inspecting it reads 8.6 MB of the file.
- A 268,439,553-byte attachment, added and extracted through file streams, at most 0.30 MiB, with matching hashes.
- A 500-page form with 1,000 fields and object streams: extraction, edit, and sign, at most 10.31 MiB. All 1,000 fields match, and `qpdf --check` passes.

The Technical Lead re-ran `net8.0` with matching numbers. Limitations: the suite asserts the D-20 ceilings, not the tighter budgets, because the heap counter is process-wide. Timing is from one machine, in Debug with coverage on. The object-stream file allocates 90–175 MiB per operation through repeated decoding (O1). These G7 criteria are not covered: repeated use and dispose, encrypted xref-stream discovery, output and decompression limits, and a timing baseline.
