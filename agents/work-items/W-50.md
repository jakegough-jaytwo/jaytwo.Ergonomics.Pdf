# W-50. Bound oversize literal serialization allocation

## Problem

The serializer allocates roughly seven to twelve times the size of an oversize literal before refusing it; the eight MiB appendix bound currently constrains the buffer rather than all transient serialization work.

Measured 2026-10-21 on `net10.0` at commit `fb71c8d`, serializing one 9 MiB `PdfObject.Str` to `Stream.Null` with `GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread`: a printable literal allocates 5.00 times its length, a literal of escaped bytes 10.01 times, and a hex string 10.00 times. `WriteString` builds the whole encoded `string` (`PdfTextCodec.EscapeLiteral` or `ToUpperHex`) and then a whole ASCII `byte[]` before the first byte reaches the output. An encrypted string first allocates its ciphertext, twice on the AES path, and is then hex-encoded the same way. The reserved `/Contents` gap is written as one `string` of zeros and its ASCII copy. `PdfAppendixBuffer` refuses only once those copies exist, so `ACCEPTANCE.md` says its 8 MiB bound is on the buffer only, not on the serializer's working set.

## Why now

Reset plan C2 item 21, before the G7 workloads (W-79) and the G8 campaign.

## Scope and exclusions

- `PdfSerializer` writes literal strings, hex strings, and the `/Contents` gap to the output in bounded chunks. Encrypted strings are encrypted through a stream into the hex encoder, as stream bodies already are.
- The escape rules stay in one place, `PdfTextCodec`.
- The serialized bytes do not change.
- Excluded: the parser's allocation when it reads a long string (a read concern, and W-42 owns object-stream churn); `PdfEngine` patching the `/Contents` hex after signing, which is bounded by the 1 MiB reservation; the size of the appendix cap.

## Acceptance criteria

- Serializing a 9 MiB string allocates under 256 KiB, independent of its length, as a printable literal, an escaped literal, a hex string, and an RC4, AES-128, and AES-256 encrypted string. `ACCEPTANCE.md` row 41 (the 8 MiB appendix bullet) drops "That bound is on the buffer only, not on the serializer's working set." and states the serializer bound.
- Serialized bytes are unchanged: each form above round-trips byte for byte to the encoding the previous encoders produced, and the encryption, signing, and appendix-cap suites pass.

## Evidence required

Internally tested: `SerializerTests` and the existing `EncryptionTests` and `SignatureTests` suites on `net10.0`, `net8.0`, `net6.0`, and `net48`. The encrypted-string interoperability checks of acceptance-matrix row 4 (pypdf, pyHanko, `pdfsig`) re-run through `SigningInteropTests` because the string encryption path changes.

## Closeout

`PdfSerializer` writes literal strings, hex strings, and the reserved `/Contents` gap through one 4 KiB buffer. `PdfTextCodec.EscapeLiteral(byte, byte[], int)` holds the escape rules, and the whole-string `EscapeLiteral` that `AppearanceBuilder` uses now calls it. `PdfSecurity.Encrypt(byte[], int, int, Stream)` encrypts a string through `Rc4Stream` or the new `EncryptingStream`, which `Encrypt(Dict, StreamBody, Stream)` also uses, into the serializer's hex writer. The unused `Encrypt(byte[], int, int)` and `EncryptBytes` are removed.

Allocation serializing one 9 MiB string to `Stream.Null` (`GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread`, Debug build, Windows):

| Form | Before, `net10.0` | After, `net10.0` | After, `net48` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Printable literal | 47,186,328 | 2,392 | 336 |
| Escaped literal | 94,470,992 | 2,392 | 336 |
| Hex, preferred or forced by binary bytes | 94,372,184 | 272 | 336 |
| RC4 128-bit (revision 3) | 103,810,000 | 74,600 | 66,768 |
| AES-128 (revision 4) | 113,250,408 | 67,000 | 68,032 |
| AES-256 (revision 6) | 113,247,576 | 66,704 | 74,936 |
| 1 MiB `/Contents` gap | 6,291,776 | 272 | 336 |

The encrypted figures are the cipher's fixed 64 KiB chunk. None grows with the string.

Verification:

- Red: the new `SerializerTests` run against the source at `fb71c8d` on `net10.0` failed 8 of 15, every allocation case, with the "before" figures above.
- `SerializerTests` (17 cases) checks the 256 KiB bound and byte-for-byte output: literal escapes across the buffer boundary, upper-case hex past the buffer, the zero gap, each escape form, and RC4, AES-128, and AES-256 strings and AES stream bodies written in uneven pieces against `StandardEncryptor`.
- Full suite after the rebase, every external tool set (qpdf 12.4.2, Poppler 26.09.0, `pdfsig` 26.08.0, SoftHSM 2, OpenSSL 3.2.4, Python 3.13 with pypdf and pyHanko): `net10.0`, `net6.0`, and `net48` 4,277 of 4,277 passed; `net8.0` 4,276 of 4,277. It does not touch the serializer. `SigningInteropTests`, which covers the acceptance-matrix row 4 interoperability for encrypted strings, passed on every target.
- `make docker-builder docker-test` was not run; this is not an integration checkpoint.

Limitations: the parser still allocates in proportion to a long string when it reads one, and `PdfEngine` still builds the signature `/Contents` hex as a string after signing, bounded by the 1 MiB reservation. Both are outside this item.
