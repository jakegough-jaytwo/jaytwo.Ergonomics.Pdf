# W-208. Measure async-source simplification options with W-138

## Problem

Async syntax alone does not establish duplicate responsibility; removing it may harm sparse remote sources.

Source: [simplification charter](../reference/reset-siimplify.md), PLAN 17 / O 10. Ticketing baseline: `82a83855581115400d4ecb5f6884895c20ecb45b`; reverify observations at execution. This records a research question, not a verified defect.

## Closeout

Completed research on 2026-10-26 against `0b042d785e070ae8791afe6d822daec24b8e6c6e`, after W-210's completed commit. Findings, exact unadopted wording, a deterministic fixture generator, a lexical inventory, a disposable probe and execution records are under `agents/reference/simplification/W-208/`.

Recommendation: retain `OpenAsync` and `IPdfSource.ReadAsync`; do not remove them. Measured on a 52 MB, 12,000-page fixture with simulated per-read latency: async and sync opens read the same 10 ranges (1.07% of the file) and differ by about 0.2% allocation; an open cancels in under 1 ms and starts no later reads; 64 concurrent `Task.Run(Open)` took 1.2 to 1.5 s with 23 to 28 pool threads blocked versus 0.3 s and none for `OpenAsync`. Removal would also contradict Exists spec rows R13 and W20 and reverse D-55, and its savings are mostly public surface. After `OpenAsync`, 15 of 17 measured document members and every mutating editor member read synchronously (1 to 31 sequential reads, up to 1.97 MB), an async save makes one synchronous read, and an async workflow save made 32 of 92 reads synchronously; these, with the constraints a design must keep, are the evidence for W-138, which stays `proposed` and the inspection API-design owner. W-109 stays `proposed` after W-138; no decode counts were measured.
