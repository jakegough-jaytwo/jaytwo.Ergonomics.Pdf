## Compatibility, credentials and recovery

Business value today: saving filled government forms in older Reader, using embedded-file privileges, and signing pre-existing fields while keeping issuer-granted capabilities. Rights removal deliberately gives those capabilities up. Table 255 names FullSave, form import/export, submission, annotation, attachment and signature privileges; which a particular Reader actually offers depends on its version, defaults and acceptance of the issuer. Modern saving support must not be extrapolated to old Readers.

No legacy Reader was exercised. The conventional installation probe found `Acrobat.exe` version **24.5.20414.0**, not a legacy Reader test environment; the conventional Reader executable path was absent. Directory names alone are not evidence of an installed executable. Modern Acrobat was not launched. There is no before/after fill-save-reopen, attachment or signing UI evidence for any Reader version, and no claim of silent downgrade, compatibility or recovery of viewer privileges. A licensed/access-controlled old viewer plus a real valid rights-enabled fixture and permitted isolated environment would be needed for that claim; do not delay the evidence-backed credential conclusion or imply this missing check passed.

Recommended simplification candidate: unsigned rights-bearing nonempty mutations refuse until explicit removal; already signed/certified documents retain D-45's existing rights-preservation exception and all other operation gates. This removes the grant interpreter at the cost of refusing previously granted unsigned fills, attachment edits and existing-field signing. Reads/export remain available. Assembly's rights refusal is coordinated with W-197; no new derivative is authorized here. Retaining the current grant interpreter is a viable lower-migration-cost alternative, and remains the shipped behavior until approval.

## Savings, retained work and recommendation

Delete the three private grant/version/right-list interpreters (56 source lines at this baseline), their category/right argument plumbing, automatic-removal branches and grant-specific test expectations. Replace fourteen call sites with the unsigned-rights refusal gate; retain signed-state detection and permission checks. Reuse direct/indirect dictionary removal through the existing backup boundary. Retain/add public detection, explicit editor removal, malformed-input handling, cache correctness, operation rollback, signing-before-first-signature gating, diagnostics, prefix/encryption tests and documentation. Appearance generation, field validation, DocMDP/FieldMDP, signature inspection, assembly admission, pending-edit state and physical-output rollback remain. Do not delete or weaken these to claim savings.

Engineering judgment: gross removal about 70-120 runtime lines; 60-130 lines added for public/API docs, refusal and explicit preparation plumbing, so net may be **60 lines added to 60 removed**. Counts are a scope estimate, not measured implementation or a performance claim. Estimate 2-4 engineer-days for rights API/policy/tests/docs, plus 1-2 joint days for XFA ordering and credential/corpus checks. The joint 1-2 days is the same allowance as W-198, counted once. W-198 owns XFA implicit-conversion deletion; W-199 alone owns all shared grant-interpreter savings. Integrated workflow preparation adds distinct ownership/publication work and is excluded.

Recommend submitting the explicit policy as an optional Gate 2 slice with the fill-only regression in usability clearly disclosed; do not describe it as behavior-preserving cleanup or as an established compatibility improvement. Keep the existing grant-aware policy if that lost workflow/legacy support is unacceptable. No current defect was confirmed, so no runtime fix or permanent product regression test is added by this research. Missing legacy-viewer evidence prevents a compatibility promise; implementation approval must explicitly accept that limitation or require that evidence first.
