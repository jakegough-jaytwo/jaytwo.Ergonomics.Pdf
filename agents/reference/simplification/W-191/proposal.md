# W-191 approval packet: tag admission, preservation, explicit loss

Status: **recommendation pending owner approval** (O 02 / O 12, D-66). These are draft replacement/additional clauses, not adopted spec text or executable W items. No new public names are reserved. W-194/W-197 retain signatures and general admission; this packet cannot bypass their decisions. Sources and measured limitations: [findings.md](findings.md).

## Exact proposed eventual contract

Replace only the tag portion of phase-2 section 3, item 4 under deliberate loss, and A4; reconcile A5's discarded-tree statement with the bounded inspection below. Update ACCEPTANCE and behavior documentation in the implementing item after owner adoption. Preserve all unrelated A3/A4 exclusions until separately approved.

> Assembly preserves the supported logical structure of selected tagged pages. Unsupported or ambiguous structure within the declared tag-inspection scope refuses with PdfAssemblyNotSupportedException before destination output begins; exceeding a shared resource budget raises PdfResourceLimitException. No successful fallback discards accessibility structure. The operation does not repair tags, infer reading order from painted content, or assert PDF/UA conformance or complete accessibility.

> The first supported subset is PDF 1.x page-local structure: a StructTreeRoot with one Document wrapper per input, whose ordered child blocks each refer exclusively to one source page; nested ordinary structure elements within each block; integer MCID children or MCR dictionaries using effective Pg and no Stm/StmOwn; and OBJR references to supported retained page annotations or ordinary form widgets. Grouping within a page is retained. Blocks and their descendants must have a unique structural parent and resolvable references; P backreferences are permitted, K cycles and multiply owned children refuse. A container spanning pages, a semantic unit partly excluded by selection, or an originally empty semantic element outside this subset refuses rather than being split or pruned by guesswork.

> Source logical sibling order is retained within each page instance. The caller's selected page-instance order determines the order of the page-local blocks in that input's Document wrapper; input occurrence order determines wrapper order. Selecting a page twice clones its blocks twice. Selecting no page contributes no tag wrapper, but retains the separately documented whole-input admission rules. Unselected entire page-local blocks are excluded by selection; malformed or unsupported source tag structures are not reclassified as selected-page exclusion. This is an explicit assembly ordering rule, not a statement that page order always equals source reading order.

> Page content remains encoded and MCIDs remain unchanged. Output page instances receive fresh StructParents keys and rebuilt ParentTree associations, preserving sparse array slots and null holes. Referenced content identifiers must be nonnegative integers and structurally consistent with ParentTree. No allocation is based solely on the largest unchecked key or MCID. Every output tag Pg and OBJR Obj names the corresponding output instance. Each retained annotated object has a fresh StructParent key where required. Immutable streams and resources may be shared, but repeated pages or input occurrences never share page/annotation ownership associations.

> Tag attributes supported initially are S, P, K, Pg, Lang, Alt, ActualText, T, C and ordinary A attribute dictionaries with primitive/array values and no document/object references. RoleMap custom names must resolve to supported PDF 1.x standard structure types without cycles. ClassMap entries must be supported attribute dictionaries or arrays of them; C is a name or array of names without revision-number qualifiers. For this subset, S may be a standard PDF 1.x type or a resolvable custom name. Unknown semantic keys or variants, revision attributes, Namespaces, IDTree, structure IDs/Ref relationships, referenced tagged content streams, or unresolved attributes refuse. This is a deliberately narrow preservation boundary; unknown keys are not silently stripped.

> RoleMap and ClassMap definitions used by retained elements are preserved. Equal names from different inputs must have equal semantic definitions in the supported subset; conflicting definitions refuse before output. No first-input-wins rule applies. Equality is structural over the supported primitive values, arrays and dictionaries, independent of dictionary key ordering and source object identity; ambiguity or a budget overrun fails closed. Attribute/class revision mechanisms are outside the initial subset. Language inherited from an input catalog is materialized on its Document wrapper; element language overrides, alternate descriptions, replacement text and supported attributes are preserved. No single catalog language substitutes for different input languages.

> A retained annotation or widget referenced by structure cannot be successfully omitted by an appearance or link rule in tag-preserving assembly. Unsupported meaningful relationships refuse, including OBJR to an unretained annotation on a selected page, rather than leaving dangling structure. Supported widgets keep the occurrence field identity described by the form contract, with distinct widget/tag ownership on each output page instance. No automatic flattening occurs.

> Tag inspection covers Catalog StructTreeRoot, MarkInfo, Lang and their supported structural closure, including ParentTree, RoleMap and ClassMap, together with selected page and retained annotation/resource structural tag keys. A present but unresolved tree, Marked true without a usable tree, or reachable StructParent/StructParents without a usable association refuses. Marked false does not suppress inspection of a present tree. The first preservation subset refuses a mixture of tagged and untagged nonempty occurrences or an unassociated selected page in a tagged occurrence; it does not invent tags for untagged content. Tag graphs and output-instance expansion charge the existing aggregate structural-byte, object, depth and output-page budgets. No whole-file repair scan or page-content interpretation is performed. Content-only hidden tagging, actual MCID occurrence/completeness, meaningful alternative descriptions, correct table semantics and accessibility of the source are not established by these checks.

> Sources remain unchanged. Destination validation, authentication, encryption consent, permissions, signature/certification policy, source integrity, destination-write failures and workflow file publication retain their separately documented guarantees. Completing structural preflight does not guarantee that later reads or destination writes cannot fail.

This first slice is narrow by design: a multi-page paragraph is legitimate PDF but outside it. Cross-page source `cross-page.pdf` must refuse even for full selection, not get repackaged as independent paragraphs. Support for tables or lists crossing pages needs an additional scope and semantic evidence. The subset's usefulness on W-189's eight successful tagged real files is unmeasured and must be evaluated before claiming broad business compatibility. W-197 may propose a different whole-input/selected-closure rule; that requires reconciliation, not silent precedence here.

## Independently approvable temporary admission wording

> Until the approved preservation subset ships, assembly refuses an input occurrence with any present StructTreeRoot, MarkInfo.Marked true, or a structural tag-parent key in the declared selected-page/retained-object inspection scope. A malformed indicator fails closed. The typed refusal occurs before destination output. A false or absent Marked value alone does not establish an untagged source. These enumerated structural checks do not validate accessibility or inspect content bytes. There is no automatic untagged fallback.

This option converts 8/30 W-189 accepted default inputs into refusals and does not implement tag preservation. Recommend only as an explicitly adopted transition with a linked preservation item; do not label the permanent product untagged-only or make those eight a customer-demand metric. Existing rejection reasons can occur earlier. Empty selections and whole-input inspection are reconciled with W-197 before adoption.

## Separately approvable explicit derivative wording

> A separately authorized assembly terminal may create a deliberately untagged new-document derivative for visual distribution when the caller explicitly accepts loss of logical reading order, structure relationships, alternative descriptions and other tag-based functionality. It is never invoked after preservation fails without new caller authorization. It omits StructTreeRoot, RoleMap/ClassMap and structure ownership associations, clears tagged status, and retains supported page content encoded; encoded marked-content operators may remain. The derivative is not sanitization, accessibility remediation, or a claim that no tag-related bytes remain. Its source is unchanged.

> Derivative authorization applies only to accessibility loss. It does not authorize dropping other meaningful features, unsigned/certified derivatives, rights removal, abandoning XFA, bypassing authentication/permissions, or relaxing any separately adopted admission rule. Ordinary editable forms and supported annotations retain their approved semantics. The result gives a bounded explicit untagged-derivative indicator; it does not manufacture tag preservation success or require per-page loss histories. Failure and publication guarantees remain those of assembly.

Recommend deferring this terminal until W-197 identifies a real recovery workflow and the common authorization/failure model. If adopted, make it a small named assembly operation, not RemoveTags, StripUnsupported, BestEffort or a per-subtype flag. A caller who needs accessible output must obtain a supported tagged source or arrange separate remediation; this derivative cannot satisfy that need.
