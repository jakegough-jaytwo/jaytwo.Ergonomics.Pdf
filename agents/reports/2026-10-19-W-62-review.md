### 2. `QUALIFICATION.proposed.md` G4: separate positive, tamper, and refusal outcomes

Line 79 includes positive signed outputs, tampered documents, prohibited writes, and signer failure in one scenario list. Line 83 then requires "every scenario" to pass both verifiers. A prohibited write or signer failure produces no signature to verify, while a tampered signature is supposed to fail verification. The gate is therefore not literally satisfiable as written.

Replace the first clause of line 83, `every scenario passes both verifiers`, with:

> every positive signed-output scenario passes both verifiers; both verifiers reject each tampered case; refusal and failure scenarios meet their prescribed negative assertions without producing a completed output

Keep the remainder of line 83 unchanged.

### 3. `QUALIFICATION.proposed.md` G3: carry all of plan C4's cross-cutting cases

Line 71 carries most of C4, but it does not require sync/async equivalence and describes usage-rights coverage only as behavior "where it applies." C4 specifically requires permitted, absent, unresolvable, malformed, unknown, and insufficient `/UR3` cases, plus sync/async equivalence and cancellation atomicity. Those are objective checks and should not disappear from the qualification gate.

Append to line 71:

> Usage-rights cases include permitted, absent, unresolvable, malformed, unknown, and insufficient `/UR3`; except for a structural parse failure, every non-permitting case removes `/UR3` in the same revision and proceeds when password permissions, DocMDP, and FieldMDP otherwise allow the write. Async writes also demonstrate sync/async equivalence and cancellation leaving the destination unchanged.

### 4. `QUALIFICATION.proposed.md` G9: remove the failure-atomicity qualifier

Line 130 says a failed or cancelled write leaves the destination unchanged only "where that is guaranteed." That preserves the old qualification document's hedge, but it conflicts with Phase 1 tenet 3 (failure never leaves a half-written output), spec G9 (every failure path leaves output unchanged), and plan C4 (destination unchanged on every documented failure).

Replace:

> a failed or cancelled write leaves the destination unchanged where that is guaranteed, and the guarantee is stated accurately

with:

> a failed or cancelled write leaves the destination unchanged

## Author response (2026-10-20)

Findings 1, 2, and 3 are accepted with your exact text, and applied to the drafts. ### Finding 4: counterproposal

I agree the old hedge ("where that is guaranteed") has to go. It would let any failure path off the hook. But the unconditional "a failed or cancelled write leaves the destination unchanged" promises something no stream library can guarantee. When the destination stream itself fails part-way through a write (an I/O error, a full disk, a broken network stream), the bytes already accepted cannot be taken back by the library. It can only try to restore the original length, and that call can fail on the same broken stream. An unconditional gate would be either unsatisfiable or quietly ignored, and either is worse than an exact rule.

Every other failure is still covered without exception: input and parse failures, validation and permission refusals, size limits, signer and TSA failures, and cancellation, all detected or handled before the appendix reaches the destination, as the shipped "failed signs do not write" behavior already does.

Applied text in G9:

> a failed or cancelled write leaves the destination unchanged, except when the destination stream itself fails (for example an I/O error or a full disk), in which case the library attempts to restore the destination's original length, rethrows, and `README.md` documents that this restoration cannot be guaranteed;

This is consistent with spec W19, which lists signer, timestamp, size, and validation failures. Tenet 3 ("failure never leaves a half-written output") reads more broadly than this exception, so I will flag the exception to the owner as a clarification of the tenet, not a change to the spec's requirements. If you prefer the exception to require that the restoration attempt be tested (a destination stream that fails after N bytes, checked for restored length when `SetLength` still works), I would add that sentence.

Please reply below, appending at the end of the file.
