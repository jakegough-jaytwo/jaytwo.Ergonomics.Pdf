**Residual trade-off, approved by the owner with D4:** one document can flatten under the legacy path and refuse under a strict path because of NoZoom, or because of an unpreservable `/OC`. The corpus has no NoZoom widget (0 of 861) and its 4 `/OC` widgets are ordinary group references; both refusals are explicit, restore the call and never affect legacy flatten.

**Unexecuted FLATTEN-C1 to C7.** C1: an `/OC` widget flattened by both paths keeps its group membership (BDC/EMC and Properties), so toggling the group in a reader hides it with the page content. C2: a dangling or non-group `/OC` refuses on the strict path and restores the call, while the legacy outcome equals a pre-change compiled client's. C3: NoRotate on a rotated page refuses on both paths; on an unrotated page both place it. C4: NoZoom paints on the legacy path and refuses on the strict path. C5: Hidden and NoView widgets are removed unpainted on both. C6: the missing-appearance rule omits a no-colour blank widget and refuses a coloured one or one whose non-Off AS names a missing stream. C7: a pre-change compiled client sees identical legacy flatten successes, refusals and exception timing.

## Purpose and scope authority

Read the [charter](../charter.md) and [supported capabilities](../supported.md). Relevant scope: AcroForm population and export; AcroForm appearances; AcroForm flattening; XFA-bearing forms.

Supported text, checkbox, radio and choice fields can be populated individually or in bulk, and form values exported. Text and choice appearances use caller-supplied TrueType fonts, embedded or subset as needed, within a documented character/script subset; unsupported characters or shaping require an explicit outcome. Eligible editable fields can be flattened subject to appearance, permission and signature restrictions.

Hybrid and XFA-only forms need classification and operation-specific handling, including supported hybrid AcroForm use. XFA packet editing is excluded; any removal must be an explicitly documented consequence.

## Owner-selected contract: bulk population atomicity

On 2026-10-27, after reviewing the competitor comparison, the owner selected: "Yes—make each bulk call all-or-nothing." This approves the following failure boundary within this draft; it does not adopt the entire form contract or authorize runtime changes.

**FORM-BULK-01.** A bulk form population call is all-or-nothing. If the call fails, the pending document state is restored to its state immediately before that call. Successful edits made before the call remain pending; failure does not discard them. A successful call applies the accepted field changes from that call as a unit.

Atomicity applies to the call, not to a sequence of separate population calls or a whole workflow. This clause does not settle which inputs are accepted, missing/duplicate-field behavior, value conversion, appearance support, cancellation observation points, exception types, external side effects or destination/publication guarantees. The accepted inputs, missing and duplicate names, value conversion, appearance support, cancellation observation and exception types are FORM-TARGET-01, FORM-BULK-02, FORM-VALUE-01, FORM-APPEARANCE-02, RI-2 and RI-1; do not infer them from this atomicity decision.

### Acceptance scenarios for FORM-BULK-01

These are draft verification scenarios, not executed results or a claim of current coverage:

- Start with a previously successful pending edit. Submit a bulk call containing a mixture that must fail under the eventual validation rules. Confirm that none of this call's changes remain and the prior edit is unchanged.
- Inject an operation failure after processing has begun, using an appropriate test seam once the implementation is assessed. Confirm the same pending-state restoration rather than testing only errors detected before any work.
- Submit an accepted bulk call and verify all its requested changes. Then fail a later bulk call and confirm that the earlier successful call remains pending.

Map eventual executable checks to FORM-BULK-01 and the chosen input/failure rules. The historical W-68 evidence is a starting point for coverage assessment, not a newly executed pass.

### R1 consolidated complete-value layout profile

**Owner-selected draft direction.** An explicit appearance-ready request succeeds only when every changed widget represents the complete accepted value. Positive caller size takes precedence over valid positive effective DA size and stays fixed. Only single-line DA-zero with no positive override searches: the caller supplies a finite maximum of at least 6 pt; eligible sizes are multiples of 0.1 pt from 6.0 through that maximum rounded down. Choose the largest eligible size passing all fit checks, and refuse if none passes. No 24-pt default or multiline DA-zero automatic sizing is introduced. Glyph-free clears follow FORM-FONT-02. Existing engine `Fill` and workflow `Fill`/`FillForm`, including successful `NeedsViewer`, retain their behavior and exception timing.

Use a rectangular widget with finite positive local dimensions. Absent `/MK` or `/MK/R` resolves to zero; supported resolved local rotations are 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees. An invalid present rotation or unresolvable geometry gives unsupported-profile. Exchange display width/height at 90/270, lay out in display coordinates, and explicitly transform the complete appearance into the physical widget box; reader rotation or extra border painting cannot supply missing room.

Support borderless, solid, dashed, underline, beveled and inset with finite nonnegative width W. Reserve the whole inside-painted stroke on each solid/dashed side, or the bottom side for underline. Beveled/inset reserve 2W on every display side even when the outer W ring in `/MK/BC` is omitted because BC is absent; paint a W shading band inside that ring. Following local rotation, beveled display top/left is white, bottom/right is `/MK/BG` component-wise halved (0.5 grey if BG absent); inset top/left is 0.5 grey, bottom/right 0.75 grey. This is the selected PDFium-convention profile, not a claim of reader-regeneration conformance. A border effect other than none, unknown style, nonrectangular clip or undeterminable footprint gives unsupported-profile. Refuse nonpositive usable content width/height.

At least 1 pt of clear space separates the content box from the reserved border footprint on every display side, or from the widget edge when borderless. Every accepted line's emitted advance span, line box and final transformed glyph ink must lie inside that box. Include negative bearings, descenders and all leading, repeated and trailing ordinary-space/NBSP advances, even where they paint no ink. Explicit blank lines consume line boxes. Adjacent-line ink must not overlap. FORM-TEXT-05 governs preserved CR/LF/CRLF, space-preferred wrapping and character fallback without splitting NBSP-joined runs. Wrapping must retain every character and space advance; no trimming, substitution, truncation, clipping or viewer regeneration establishes success.

Single-line text is centred vertically; multiline starts at the content top. Centre alignment centres the full emitted advance span. Left/right alignment places a line as close to that edge as keeps both its advance span and ink inside the content box; move inward for edge ink overhang. Glyph overhang alone never makes otherwise fitting text ineligible; a genuinely too-wide combined advance/ink span still refuses. Exact placement is documented behavior rather than a contract number.

Fit is conservative: no tolerance accepts overflow. Within a fit margin below 0.01 pt the library may refuse; it never accepts overflow. Results are deterministic per input and library version. Fit uses the same advances, positions and selected size actually emitted; arithmetic method and internal precision remain engineering choices. Insets absorb reader placement error; the refuse-only margin is not an accepting tolerance.

**Initial behavior proposal under owner decision 4, not implemented behavior:** 1 pt clear space, selected font `hhea` ascent minus descent plus nonnegative line gap for line progression, with every explicit blank line consuming that progression. Use ascent/descent for line boxes, centre the single-line box vertically, and start the first multiline box at the content top. Exact clear space, metrics, baseline placement and rounding may evolve between versions while all public guarantees hold; there is no public 0.001-pt grid or mandatory outward-rounding algorithm. Record the implementation's actual choices when implemented.

**Evidence and unexecuted acceptance:** independent exact-width/beveled fixtures and Arial/Times Italic outline arithmetic support the geometry investigation only; see the [research record](drafting/research/w218-appearance/README.md). ## R2. Readiness after Save and reopen

**Exact proposed wording:**

> Readiness describes a specific value, widget set and appearance state, not the fact that a Save completed. ### R2 appendix: requested-validation oracle (proposal, 2026-10-31)

This specifies a read-only operation, not viewer repair, general AP interpretation or automatic validation on Open/Save. Its scope is the requested logical fields and every relevant widget of each field, resolved afresh after reopen. A result identifies its scope, accepted semantic value, widget state and reason; it is valid only for that observed state.

| State | Required observation |
| --- | --- |
| `Unknown` | No validation has run for the current value/widget/AP state, or an edit has invalidated its proof. This is the initial state after reopen, including files saved after a ready result. |
| `Ready` | Every required widget has proven value-matching paint under the finite supported profile, or a proven eligible blank/no-visible-paint result. For declared-state buttons the proof is correct state binding and the artwork is not evaluated; for list boxes it is the visible-row construction under the list rule. |
| `Unready` | A definite counterexample exists: required paint absent, established stale value/state, incorrect selected state, clipped required paint or a known data-only/legacy NeedsViewer pending condition. Include the failing widget and reason. |
| `Indeterminate` | Validation ran but cannot establish agreement or a definite counterexample, including an unsupported AP program, unavailable provenance, ambiguous association or unsupported profile. Include a reason such as `UnsupportedProfile` or `ValueMatchNotEstablished`; never promote it to Ready. Malformed required input and source I/O use R4's controlled failures. |

**Provenance/value-match procedure.** Resolve effective field type, exact semantic value, selected options/button state, widget ownership, visibility, geometry/rotation and referenced appearance/font resources. Establish the normal AP selected for each widget, including AS where required. Recognize only a finite supported appearance construction whose complete value-dependent paint can be accounted for. Compare its decoded glyph mapping, serialized advances and positions, line/cell/option placement, clipping/transforms and selected state against the current value and R1/R4 profile. Account for every value-bearing paint operation and every required value item; a matching substring, ToUnicode text alone, stream hash alone or an AP's presence is not proof. Independent decoration must be separable from value paint. Unsupported operators/resources or unexplained paint make the result indeterminate, even when a viewer looks plausible.

For a current-session generated AP, provenance binds the accepted semantic value, complete widget set/state, profile, AP bytes and all referenced resources to the result. Any relevant later change invalidates that proof. After reopen, re-derive the same facts from the serialized document using the supported recognizer; do not trust an old in-memory token, producer name, private marker or hash without rechecking the semantic binding. No new persisted marker is required. A ready-origin file may therefore become indeterminate after reopen if its construction is outside the recognizer; no guarantee of arbitrary-program validation is made. A stored AP and a false/absent NeedAppearances flag do not demonstrate value agreement. A true flag likewise does not prove that a particular widget's paint is wrong; it prevents treating a document-wide flag as field-level proof.

For a blank/no-paint case, prove the accepted empty/unset semantics under the field-family contract, the absence of current inherited nonblank content and stale value paint, and the eligibility of any hidden/nonpainting widget treatment under the separate flatten contract. Missing AP by itself is insufficient. Validation never generates paint, changes V/RV/AP/AS/resources/flags, writes output or saves; compare the complete pending state before/after both success and failure. If a usable source is retained, a subsequent explicit Save must observe the same pending edits. I/O position restoration is outside this oracle.

#### List fields (resolved proposal, W-219)

**Owner-confirmed 2026-10-31 as D2; list fixtures remain required post-adoption evidence.** This applies to an explicit appearance-ready fill or clear of a list box (Combo unset). A data-only call writes V and I only and leaves `TI` unchanged. Legacy `Fill` is unchanged.

1. Resolve Opt, V and I under the choice clauses. Let `n` be the number of complete option rows that fit the R1 content box (content height divided by the line progression, rounded down). If `n` is zero, refuse as an unsupported profile.
2. With no selected row, keep `TI` (absent means 0) and paint the visible rows without selection backgrounds.
3. Otherwise let `s` be the lowest selected index. If `TI <= s < TI + n`, keep `TI`. Else write a local `TI` equal to `min(s, max(0, optionCount - n))`. `TI` is scroll position, not form data. The change is part of the call's pending state and is restored on failure like any other widget change.
4. Paint each visible row's full display label (never its export string), with no wrapping and no reordering, and a selection background behind each visible selected row. A selected row outside the visible interval, as in a multi-selection spread wider than the viewport, is not painted; its selection stays in V/I and the result reports it by index. No selection is dropped from storage.
5. Foreground and selection colours and row spacing are versioned documented behavior, not contract values.

**Why.** Refusing when a selection is scrolled out of view would refuse the first fill of any long list whose choice is not near the top, which is a common case, to protect a scroll position no user stores on purpose. Every reader repaints a list from `TI`, so choosing `TI` is the simplest predictable way to make the filled value visible. **Evidence.** None is empirical: the 33-PDF corpus has no list box, and no list fixture was rendered. The rule is inference from the ISO definition of `TI` and the R1 line-box model.

**Unexecuted LIST-C1 to C3** (fixtures to be built, since the corpus has none): C1, selected row already visible, `TI` unchanged; C2, selected row below the viewport, `TI` follows the formula including the last-page clamp, and a late failure restores `TI`; C3, a multi-selection spread wider than the viewport stores complete V/I, paints only visible rows and does not refuse, while a content box shorter than one row refuses.

#### AP recognition construction and proof

Recognize parsed PDF tokens, not byte substrings or producer-specific spacing. Whitespace/comments, literal versus hexadecimal strings and resource names may differ without changing the recognized construction. Each admitted stream is one Form XObject with positive finite BBox, its own resolved Resources, identity or an explicitly accounted-for Matrix, and the 12.5.5 BBox/Matrix-to-Rect mapping. Compute the resulting transform; do not apply MK/R twice. The implicit Form BBox clip exists even when the stream contains no clipping operator: all required ink must fit inside it as well as R1's content box. Resource names resolve in that AP's Resources, never by substituting a same-named DR font.

The proposed text/choice construction has this ordered structure (each bracket denotes an optional or repeated *defined template*, not arbitrary PDF content):

```text
q
  state-initialization
  supported-display-transform
  [background-template] [border-template]
  /Tx BMC
    [selection-row-template]
    BT text-state-initialization
      [font-and-colour, absolute-line-or-cell-position, glyph-show-record]
    ET
  EMC
Q
```

- State initialization establishes opaque normal painting, no soft mask and no overprint, by a recognized `gs` dictionary whose effective entries are checked; it does not treat `q` as a reset. Annotation BM/CA/ca, optional content and visibility must also be accounted for. Nondefault or unresolved appearance-affecting state outside this profile yields Indeterminate, rather than assumed default paint. Unused unrelated resources need not be parsed as executable content.
- The display transform is exactly the R1 quarter-turn/translation construction composed with the Form mapping. No arbitrary nested `cm` sequence, skew or zero-scale transform is silently accepted. Background is a rectangle with a resolved device colour. Border is the exact R1 borderless/solid/dashed/underline or selected bevel/inset path template, with finite geometry, explicit `w`, `J`, `j`, `M`, `d` where applicable, and `m/l/h/re` plus `S/f/f*` as that template requires. Selection backgrounds are exact row rectangles behind their labels. Every path and colour is accounted for; arbitrary extra paths cannot be called decoration.
- Text initialization explicitly establishes `Tc=0`, `Tw=0`, `Tz=100`, `Tr=0`, `Ts=0` and the line-spacing state actually used. `BT` does not reset these parameters. `Tf` chooses one proved embedded static TrueType binding and finite positive size per run. `Tm` establishes each line/cell origin; `Tj` or `TJ` carries complete glyph codes, with each TJ adjustment reconciled to the selected layout. No hidden text rendering mode, inherited spacing, approximate kerning, nested BT, unbalanced q/Q/BMC/EMC, leftover operands or trailing unaccounted tokens is accepted. CR/LF controls and soft wraps are reconstructed from the stored value and layout; they need not have painted glyph codes.
- Initial font-resource proof covers one horizontal Type0 Identity-H font with CIDFontType2 descendant, embedded FontFile2, resolved widths and CIDToGIDMap (stream or Identity), and a finite ToUnicode map for the used codes. Reconcile code -> CID -> glyph ID, serialized widths/positions, retained Unicode cmap -> actual glyph ID and glyph outlines/metrics. A ToUnicode assertion alone is insufficient. Require the subset to retain enough Unicode/glyph correspondence for this independent check; absence yields Indeterminate after reopen. Equivalent simple-font or other CMap programs are outside this initial recognizer, not declared invalid PDFs. Composite glyphs need controlled structural processing under decision 8.
- Account for every visible scalar/space advance and every line/cell/option in order. R1's advances, ink, line boxes, overhang placement and no-overlap/containment rules still apply. Compare option display labels rather than export keys; compare exact selected-row markers and all required widgets. Font cmap/outline agreement establishes the declared glyph correspondence, not a general judgment of a font designer's visual letter shapes.

This ordered grammar admits `/Tx BMC`/`EMC` (missing from the earlier whitelist), state-setting and border dash operators. It excludes explicit `W/W*`, arbitrary `Do`, inline images, shading/pattern paint, Type3 fonts, executable XObject nesting, arbitrary ExtGState entries and property-bearing marked content. These are recognizer limits, not format prohibitions. Annotation actions are preserved and never executed; their presence alone does not make a static AP unreadable. A script-dependent or alternate interactive presentation is not part of the proved normal appearance. No script output is guessed.

A supported construction with a definite value/state mismatch or overflow is Unready. A well-formed construction outside the grammar is Indeterminate with UnsupportedProfile; it remains inspectable. Malformed required structure or failed source access follows R4's controlled failure categories. AppearanceReady mutation and flatten requiring proof refuse on either nonready outcome, restoring their own call; validation itself never changes the PDF. Declared button-state binding is reported separately: it is the readiness proof for declared-state buttons, and their artwork is not evaluated. The narrow generated blank-checkbox/no-paint case can use the defined decoration-only construction. [Button readiness](#button-readiness-declared-state-binding-resolved-proposal-w-219) below replaces the earlier path-only artwork candidate.

**Unexecuted scenarios AP-C1?C6:** recognized text with Tx markers and an equivalent hex string; nonidentity Matrix paired with MK/R to detect double rotation; identical glyph bytes with changed CIDToGIDMap/widths/cmap or lying ToUnicode; inherited nonzero spacing/invisible text mode or annotation opacity; extra paint after a matching value, and an arbitrary button mark with otherwise correct AS; implicit BBox clipping with no W operator. Pair each negative with a supported control. Save/reopen must rederive the proof, with no retained-token shortcut. Reader agreement and token matching alone do not satisfy these scenarios.

##### State and mapping proof obligations

**Engineering proposal; no new acceptance gate adopted.** Carry a state record through each recognized token, with an empty operand/path stack at template boundaries. `q` copies state, `Q` restores it, and `BT` resets only its specified matrices. Before any paint, every parameter that the template uses must be established by the applicable appearance invocation rules or an explicit checked setter. A `gs` changes only entries actually supplied; it is not a whole-state reset. Include Group/compositing, annotation opacity/blend, transfer-function overrides, overprint and resource `DefaultGray`/`DefaultRGB`/`DefaultCMYK` substitutions in the dependency closure when applicable. A nondefault dependency outside the proof yields Indeterminate, not an assumption of device-default paint. Do not reject a valid harmless unused resource merely because it is present. Sources: both editions 8.4.5, 8.6.5.6, 8.10 and 12.5.5; PDF 2.0 Table 57 and Table 93.

Use the actual four BBox corners under Matrix to derive the enclosing upright transformed box, then derive A from that box to Rect and compose in the specification's order. Degenerate/nonfinite mappings cannot supply a fit proof. Intersect the transformed implicit BBox clip with each admitted local clip and the promised content region; the absence of explicit W is irrelevant. Account separately for page rotation and annotation NoRotate/NoZoom behavior; if the selected presentation cannot be proved, report Indeterminate. A normal-appearance proof concerns the supported annotation presentation, not visibility against arbitrary later page/annotation paint or every possible zoom. Flatten must separately establish the equivalent page placement and any affected visibility/printing semantics; copying the stream bytes alone is insufficient.

For every glyph-show record, retain the complete chain from serialized code boundaries through CMap, CID, CIDToGIDMap, font glyph and Unicode correspondence. Check declared widths against the actual positioning model, including word/character spacing and TJ adjustments. Prove painted outline bounds and space advances; glyph count or extracted Unicode equality is not a substitute. Permit only already admitted font/construction families; this proof does not add simple fonts, Type3 or shaping. A later glyph, path, image or nested invocation cannot be ignored after finding a matching value. These obligations refine the existing proof; feasibility and representative-input acceptance remain unverified.

##### Button readiness: declared-state binding (resolved proposal, W-219)

**Owner-confirmed 2026-10-31 as D1.** For a checkbox or radio whose widgets declare normal-state appearances, AppearanceReady means binding, not artwork. #### Rich blank construction (resolved proposal, W-219)

**Engineering proposal; applies to the explicit paired rich clear (FORM-RICH-02/03).** On the terminal field dictionary only: write V as a present empty text string, remove the field's own RV entry, and change nothing else in the field. **Executed VALUE-C6 (2026-10-31; partial, one row-selecting reader).** The [script](drafting/research/w218-choice-language-experiments.py) hand-writes 30 single-field PDFs (paired, shared-export, shared-label and empty-export Opt; combo, editable combo, list, MultiSelect; V as export or display; absent, consistent and contradictory I) and reads them with pypdf 6.21.4 (raw), PDFium 156.0.8076.0 (form environment, per-row selection) and MuPDF 1.28.5 (raw V and option pairs only: it exposes no row selection and its binding returns an empty value for array V, so it neither confirms nor contradicts row semantics). Observed in PDFium: a paired row is selected only when V equals its export string, never when V holds the display name; with I present V still takes precedence, including when V matches no row; a shared export with no I selects every sharing row but with I=[1] selects only row 1, and only when V is the export; I alone selects its row; an empty-string V selects an empty-export row while an absent V selects none; a single-select I on a plain list is harmless; a literal not in Opt selects no row. Details: [record](drafting/research/w218-choice-language-experiments.json). **Resolved by this evidence:** the I-versus-V precedence and the I-only/null/empty controls behave as the table above proposes in the one row-selecting reader. **Not resolved:** Acrobat, pdf.js, PDFBox and strict-validator behaviour were not run; a writer or reader following the ISO prose (display-name V) disagrees with PDFium, so export-string V is a documented interoperability choice with a residual risk for display-matching readers. Reads therefore accept a row only when V matches exactly one row's export string, or exactly one row's display string when no export matches; if V matches different rows by export and by display, or several rows without a disambiguating I, the selected value is not established and a complete-form read or export refuses under the settled boundary. A tool's self-round-trip remains one observation, not format validity.

##### Paired choice `V`: rule and evidence separation (D3, owner-confirmed 2026-10-31)

**Rule.** Additive writes store a paired option's export string in `V` (an unpaired option's own string; an array of export strings for MultiSelect), with `I` only where the storage table requires it. Reads select a row when `V` equals exactly one row's export string, else exactly one row's display string; otherwise the selection is not established and a complete read refuses. **Compatibility consequence:** legacy `Fill` storage is unchanged by this rule; documents written by the additive path show their selection in readers that match on export values, and a reader that follows the ISO prose literally may show no selection; documents that store the display string remain readable by this library.

* **ISO wording (sourced, local text):** ISO 32000-1 Tables 230/231 and ISO 32000-2 Tables 233/234 describe a paired Opt entry as an export value and the text displayed, and the following V prose names the second member (the displayed text).
* **Observed reader behavior (executed, VALUE-C6; scope stated there):** PDFium 156 selects a paired row only when `V` is the export string and never when `V` is the display string. MuPDF and pypdf expose no row selection, so they neither confirm nor contradict. Acrobat, pdf.js, PDFBox and strict validators were not run.
* **Inference (not verified here):** the supplied review reports that PDFium's source matches against the option value and that Adobe's Acrobat Forms API treats the export value as the field value; iText and PDFBox are recalled as writing the export value. These support the rule's interoperability but were not rechecked in this pass.
* **Additional check:** none run. No available check could change the recommendation, because the only reader that selects rows disagrees with the ISO prose and the rule keeps display-string reads working. Acrobat file-level and validator checks stay post-adoption acceptance work.

##### Inherited choice clearing: resolved rule

Both editions' inheritable V and dictionary-null rules rule out deleting V or writing local null as an inheritance barrier. Writing `I []` alone also does not override V: I is the selected-index association, not a replacement for the effective value. An empty string can select an empty-export option or denote an editable empty literal, so it is not a general no-selection encoding. Neither edition's choice-value prose explicitly supplies an empty-array clear for single-select; its specified array case concerns multiple selected items. No edition-valid single-selection empty-array clear exists, so the rule below refuses that one case. Sources: ISO 32000-1 7.3.9, 12.7.3.1/Table 220, 12.7.4.4/Table 231; ISO 32000-2 7.3.9, 12.7.4.1/Table 226, 12.7.5.4/Table 234 and issue 468.

**Resolved (W-219 engineering rule, supported by the VALUE-C5 evidence below):** (a) when no ancestor supplies a value, clear by removing the local V and I; (b) a MultiSelect field with an inherited value writes local `V []` and `I []`; (c) a single-selection choice whose effective V comes from a non-terminal ancestor refuses with an unsupported-structure result that names the ancestor and changes nothing. Ancestors, siblings, DV and Opt are never touched, and the clear is never a selected empty export or an editable empty literal. This authorizes neither ancestor mutation nor restructuring.

**Executed VALUE-C5 (2026-10-31; two independent readers plus raw pypdf).** The same [script](drafting/research/w218-choice-language-experiments.py) builds a parent field with a nonblank V, a kid field A carrying each local control and an untouched sibling B, for single combos and MultiSelect lists, with and without an empty-export option (24 inherited and 7 baseline fixtures). Results: (1) local `V null` is not a portable barrier: PDFium treats it as a present empty value (A cleared), MuPDF inherits the ancestor value in the single-selection fixtures (A unchanged, as 7.3.9 omission requires; its binding reports an empty value for every array V) and pypdf keeps a null object; (2) `I []` alone changes neither effective value nor selected rows in PDFium or MuPDF; (3) `V ()` blocks inheritance but is a value: with an empty-export option PDFium selects that row, so it is not a clear; (4) local `V []` leaves A with no selected row and B with the inherited selection unchanged in PDFium (single and multiple, with or without an empty-export option), and MuPDF reports an empty value for A. **Format status:** both editions type V as a text string unless several items are selected, when it is an array of strings; an empty array is therefore within the typing only for MultiSelect, and no clause provides an empty array for a single selection. Static corpus count ([script](drafting/research/w218-inherited-v-survey.py), [record](drafting/research/w218-inherited-v-survey.json)): of 813 terminal fields in 13 AcroForm files (7 choice fields; two encrypted files skipped) none takes V from a non-terminal ancestor. **Resulting proposal:** (a) when no ancestor supplies a value, clear by removing local V and I (absence is the default no selection); (b) MultiSelect with an inherited value writes local `V []` and `I []`; (c) a single-selection choice whose effective V comes from a non-terminal ancestor has no edition-valid local no-selection encoding among the controls tried, and `V []` is reader-tolerated but outside the single-selection type. Alternatives for (c): write `V []` (non-conformant by the typing above), change the ancestor (excluded by owner direction), or refuse that one case with an unsupported-structure result naming the ancestor and changing nothing. **This revision adopts the refusal** as an engineering rule (P-CHOICE-INH). It is judged not materially narrowing, because 0 of 7 sampled choice fields inherit a value from a non-terminal ancestor, and it is listed for owner awareness under the engineering decisions recorded above. A representative input that shows otherwise returns it under A4. The corpus is small and has no list boxes. Controls with contradictory V/I, supplementary readers and Save/reopen remain unexecuted.

##### Strict text-stream decoding proposal

**Engineering proposal, not implemented.** Apply object-type eligibility first: a permitted text stream is not permission to accept streams for every string-valued key. Text-field V permits streams starting with PDF 1.5; RV permits text strings or text streams under its own edition rules; field T and choice Opt remain their specified types. Resolve/decrypt/decode a stream through the engine's ordinary stream pipeline and enforce existing actual decoded-byte limits as bytes arrive, including filter chains. No new numerical budget is selected. Do not allocate from Length or a filter's advertised size, read beyond the resolved extent or interpret stream bytes as a second PDF literal/hex string. Literal-string escapes and end-of-line normalization belong to PDF token parsing; a text stream containing backslash-n contains those two bytes, not a newline created by a second unescape pass.

Feed the resulting bytes through the INSPECT-FORM-05 exact-BOM classifier. Carry an incomplete BOM/multibyte/surrogate prefix across read boundaries; do not classify each chunk separately or emit text before encoding selection. For ordinary text segments, a recognized UTF-16BE marker requires complete code units and paired surrogates through EOF; language escapes follow the proposal below. A recognized UTF-8 marker requires the version gate and strict scalar encoding (no overlong sequences, encoded surrogates, stray continuations or values above U+10FFFF). Otherwise apply PDFDocEncoding with refusal for an undefined mapping. Do not switch encoding after a malformed selected sequence or strip a second interior BOM. Preserve CR/LF, embedded U+0000 where permitted by the owning text contract, supplementary scalars and U+00AD without normalization; P1 constrains generated paint, not complete-form reading.

Both editions 7.9.2.2 and 7.9.3 permit language escape metadata in Unicode text. PDF 2.0 separates it into 7.9.2.2.2. This is distinct from PDF backslash escaping and from literal ESC in arbitrary application text. **Language framing** is specified in the proposal below: a well-formed escape becomes a separate span rather than being silently stripped, shown as value text or globally refused; only malformed ESC use refuses a complete read. The proposal is unexecuted in the target.

**Edition-specific framing: source interpretation and reader behaviour (2026-10-31).** ISO 32000-1 7.9.2.2 (PDF pages 94-95) and ISO 32000-2 7.9.2.2.2 (page 132) define the element as U+001B (in 2.0, bytes 0,27 for UTF-16BE and the single byte 27 for UTF-8), a 2-byte language code (ISO 639 / BCP 47), an optional 2-byte ISO 3166 country code and a closing U+001B, with both editions' examples "encoded as ASCII characters". Read literally as byte counts (**reading A**), in UTF-16BE the code is two raw ASCII bytes forming one code unit (`en` is 0x656E), the whole element is 6 or 8 bytes and so preserves code-unit alignment; in UTF-8 it is ESC, two or four ASCII bytes, ESC. **Reading B** (each code letter as its own UTF-16BE unit, 4 or 8 bytes) contradicts the stated byte count and is not selected. This is source interpretation, not a reader-confirmed format fact. Neither edition gives a quoting mechanism for a literal U+001B, a code longer than two bytes or a three-letter code. Reader behaviour on the 13 [DECODE-C5 fixtures](drafting/research/w218-form-semantics.md#independent-reader-experiments-choice-storage-clearing-and-language-escapes): PDFium and MuPDF both remove well-formed reading-A elements (with or without country, adjacent runs, at text end, before a supplementary scalar) and both yield the same text; PDFium also removes reading-B and UTF-8 elements and discards all text after an unclosed or incomplete ESC, while MuPDF leaves reading-B-with-country, empty-code and unclosed ESC literally in the text and removes only the language code from the UTF-8 form; pypdf removes nothing, so reading A surfaces as characters such as U+656E, and it does not decode the UTF-8 form. The readers therefore agree only on well-formed reading A.

**Executed DECODE-C5 (2026-10-31, three readers; see above and the [record](drafting/research/w218-choice-language-experiments.json)).** Corpus: [no text-typed string](drafting/research/w218-esc-corpus-survey.json) among 5,740 strings in 31 unencrypted corpus files contains 0x1B; the 9 hits are all signature `/Contents` blobs (two encrypted files skipped).

**Language-escape proposal (newly specified; unexecuted in the target, no serialization or API adopted).** (1) Recognition runs on the selected encoding's code units or bytes before scalar decoding: U+001B opens a language escape only if the next unit (UTF-16BE) or bytes (UTF-8) are exactly two ASCII letters, optionally followed by exactly two ASCII letters, then U+001B; chunk boundaries do not change the result. (2) Any other U+001B (unclosed, wrong length, non-letter, reading-B shape, empty code) makes that string's value not established because readers disagree on it; a complete-form read or export refuses under the settled boundary, with no stripping or replacement. (3) A recognized escape yields no characters; the additive `Text` observation carries plain text plus ordered read-only language spans (start and length in UTF-16 code units of the plain text, language and optional country as ASCII); adjacent escapes are separate spans, and an escape at text end is a zero-length span. (4) Name and option matching compares plain text; a caller-supplied name has no language metadata, and annotated and plain objects with the same plain text in one matching scope are an ambiguity that refuses under the existing ambiguity rules rather than merging; inspection keeps their identities separate. (5) MaxLen counts plain-text characters, not escape bytes; no reader count was compared. (6) New writes never emit escapes, and replacing a value replaces its spans as it replaces its text; untouched fields keep their bytes. (7) A requested new value containing U+001B is not representable (no quoting mechanism exists in either edition, and PDFium loses the following text) and is refused by the additive operations; legacy Fill is unchanged. No corpus text string contains ESC, so (7) has no measured acceptance cost; if the owner disagrees it returns under A4. Residual: Acrobat was not run, MaxLen reader counting is unmeasured, and the UTF-8 branch applies only where the version gate admits it.

**Unexecuted DECODE-C1-C4:** split exact BOM and supplementary character across input chunks; truncated selected Unicode versus legal partial-BOM PDFDocEncoding controls; filtered V stream containing literal backslash-n versus a parsed literal string with a newline escape; valid language annotation versus incomplete/ambiguous ESC sequence with a nearby colliding plain name. Require no replacement alias, no partial complete-form result and no second unescape pass. The language control's expected public representation remains conditional on the unresolved policy.

##### Lossless button-state name proposal

**Engineering proposal for the additive surface.** `PdfButtonStateName` owns an immutable copy of the PDF name's unescaped octets and exposes a canonical ASCII `CanonicalName` without a leading slash. Equality and hashing use the octets, case-sensitively. Canonicalization emits bytes 0x21-0x7E literally except PDF delimiters `()<>[]{}/%` and `#`; every other byte emits `#` plus two uppercase hexadecimal digits. In particular a literal number sign emits `#23`. A canonical parser accepts exactly this spelling and produces the same bytes; no text-string BOM, UTF-8/PDFDocEncoding replacement, Unicode normalization or double-unescaping applies. Zero bytes are invalid PDF name content; an empty byte sequence is a valid name and is not the NoValue tag. The bytes of `Off` alone denote the reserved Off state; `off`, empty and other names are distinct.

For example `/A#42` and `/AB` both expose `AB`, `/A#2342` exposes `A#2342` (bytes `A#42`), and `/#FE#FF` exposes `#FE#FF` without interpreting it as a text BOM. Optional UI text may be shown only as a separately labeled strict UTF-8 observation; it never supplies identity or matching. Opt export strings remain ordinary text metadata. Additive RadioState values and declared state observations carry this type; legacy strings/APIs retain their behavior. No raw-object model or new identity-based field selector is exposed. Source: both editions 7.3.5; this canonical public spelling is library policy, not a required PDF lexical spelling.

**Unexecuted NAME-C1-C3:** equivalent escaped spellings compare equal; literal-number-sign and decoded-escape names remain distinct; high bytes, empty name and case variants round-trip through the additive observation and selected-state write without replacement or collision. Check PDF 1.0/1.1's earlier name syntax separately if those versions are admitted. Independent reader/interoperability and future compiled-client checks remain required; this proposed type alone establishes neither.

**Permission-bit source review (2026-10-31; sourced; the resulting policy is adopted in security).** ISO 32000-1 7.6.3/Table 22 (PDF pages 69-70) and ISO 32000-2 7.6.4.2/Table 22 (pages 94-95) define: bit 4, modifying contents by operations other than those controlled by bits 6, 9 and 11; bit 5, copying or extracting text and graphics (in 1.7 at revision 3 or higher excluding the accessibility extraction controlled by bit 10; in 2.0 with an assistive-technology exception); bit 6, adding or modifying text annotations and filling in form fields (creating or modifying fields additionally needs bit 4); bit 9, revision 3 or higher, filling existing fields even if bit 6 is clear; bit 10, ignored by 2.0 readers; bit 11, assembly. Adding bit 6 to the additive conjunction would deny documents that allow modification but not annotation edits and is not adopted. Whether complete-form value read or export counts as extracting text and graphics (bit 5) is answered by security, which adds no extraction-bit denial. Authentication, authorization, readiness, pending-state restoration and output guarantees stay distinct. Credentials and handler results were not executed (AUTH-C1 to C4 remain unexecuted).

**Unexecuted AUTH-C1?C4** (these add form-specific mixes to security's SECURITY-A2 to A4): R=2 bit-6 versus R>=3 bit-9-only fill; independent DocMDP/FieldMDP denial despite credentials/rights grant; flatten with fill-only authority and duplicate names under identity locks; mixed-call late failure followed by a separately failed source append.
